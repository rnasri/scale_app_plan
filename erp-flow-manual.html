0 · The Golden Path — What Must Exist Before Anything Else

Universal prerequisites, shared by every module below. Skipping or reordering these causes downstream modules to silently fail or produce wrong numbers — do this once, correctly, before any module-specific setup begins.

Why this section exists: Every module page below lists its own prerequisites, but several of those prerequisites are shared across the whole system — Company, Chart of Accounts, core masters. Setting these up separately, once per module, risks inconsistency (two different Territory trees, two different Item naming conventions). Do the Golden Path exactly once, at the very start of implementation, before touching any module-specific checklist.

1

Company

The legal entity record — name, abbreviation, default currency, country. This single record drives the default Chart of Accounts template, fiscal year defaults, and every subsequent transaction's company field. Get this right first; changing it later is disruptive.

2

Fiscal Year

The accounting period definition. Required before any transaction (Sales Invoice, Purchase Invoice, Journal Entry, Payroll run) can be created — native ERPNext blocks posting outside a defined fiscal year.

3

Chart of Accounts

Either accept ERPNext's standard template (auto-created with Company) or import the client's existing CoA. Gates every financial posting across Sales, Purchase, Payroll, and Projects — nothing "counts" financially until it can post to a real GL account. See §4 Accounts for the full build.

4

Warehouse

At least one default warehouse, even for service-only businesses — Item records need one for stock tracking, and Sales/Purchase of any stock item requires it. Multiple warehouses if the client has physical locations.

5

UOM (Unit of Measure)

Nos, Kg, Hour, Box, etc. — native defaults cover most cases; add client-specific units (e.g. "Manday," used heavily by Projects) before Item creation.

6

Item & Item Group

Every product or service the company sells or buys. Needed before a Quotation, Sales Order, Purchase Order, or BOM can reference anything. For services-only businesses, Items are still required (as non-stock Items) — Sales and Purchase transactions cannot exist without at least one Item line.

7

Territory, Customer Group, Supplier Group

The classification trees Customer and Supplier records hang off. Build the Territory tree to match the client's actual sales regions — Sales's auto-assignment logic depends directly on this being correct from day one (see §1 Sales).

8

Price Lists (Selling & Buying)

Needed before Quotation or Purchase Order pricing works. At minimum one Standard Selling and one Standard Buying price list.

9

Tax Templates (via India Compliance)

GST rates, HSN/SAC codes. Needed before any Invoice with tax can be created correctly. India Compliance is a Level 1 adopt app install, not custom code — see accounts-module.html.

10

Cost Center

Shared foundation for Accounts, Finance, and Projects budget tracking. Built once (whichever team gets there first per the AC-13/FI-05 cross-reference convention — see accounts-module.html) and consumed everywhere else with zero extra code.

11

Department & Employee master (via HR)

Needed before Timesheet, Payroll, Sales Person-to-User mapping, and Projects' Resource Allocation. This is the one Golden Path item most dependent on the client providing real external data — see §3 HR.

🔍 Understand the difference — Cost Center vs. Department vs. Territory

Three separate classification trees, easy to conflate because they all look like "org structure," but each drives a different module and answers a different question:

Cost Center
Financial dimension. Where does the money get attributed on the GL? Used by Accounts (every transaction), Finance (budget/variance), and Projects (budget tracking). Lives in the Accounting module.
Department
HR org structure. Who reports to whom, for approval routing and headcount. Used by HR (Employee, Leave approval) and Task (Department field). Not a financial dimension by itself.
Territory
Sales geography. Which region does this Customer/Lead belong to, for auto-assignment and regional reporting. Used almost exclusively by Sales — Customer, Lead, Sales Order all carry it.

A single real-world "region" or "team" often needs entries in all three trees, separately, because each one is consumed by a different module's logic — building only one and assuming the others inherit it is a common setup mistake.

🔍 Understand the difference — Customer vs. Contact vs. Address

Three separate native DocTypes that together answer "who do we sell to and where" — used identically by Sales (Customer) and Buying (the same pattern, but Supplier):

Customer
The company/account record — what gets invoiced, what carries the price list, credit limit, tax settings. One Customer can have many Contacts and many Addresses linked to it.
Contact
An individual person at that company — name, email, phone, designation. A new user often expects "Customer" to mean "the person I email," but that's actually Contact.
Address
A physical location — billing address, shipping address, office address. One Customer can have several (e.g. billing HQ vs. a delivery site), each independently linked.

After the Golden Path is done, the branches are independent: Sales needs Customer + Item + Price List + Territory. Purchase needs Supplier + Item + Price List. HR needs Department + Designation + Holiday List + Employee. Accounts needs CoA + Fiscal Year + Cost Center (largely the Golden Path itself). Finance needs Accounts substantially live first — it has no independent data source of its own. Projects needs Customer (client-facing work) + Employee (resourcing) + Cost Center (budget) + ideally a Sales Order as the commercial anchor. Manufacturing needs Item + BOM + Warehouse + Work Station. Each module section below states its own branch-specific prerequisites in full.

1 · Sales

App: scale_sales · Full detail: sales-module.html · Live tracking: sales-tracker.html

Significance

The revenue engine — Lead → Opportunity → Quotation → Sales Order → Delivery → Invoice → Collection. This is the literal core transactional loop of any commercial organization; almost everything else in the system either feeds this loop (HR provides the sales team, Accounts records the resulting revenue) or is fed by it (Finance analyzes the resulting cash flow, Projects executes what Sales sold).

Prerequisites

Golden Path items 6–9 (Item, Territory, Customer Group, Price List, Tax Templates) must exist first. No dependency on HR, Accounts, or Projects to start capturing Leads — Sales can begin generating pipeline before any other module is live, which is why it was built first.

Initial Setup Checklist
  1. Territory tree — build to match the client's actual sales regions. This system ships with 4 zones (North/South/East/West India) as a working example; auto-assignment Assignment Rules route new Leads by Territory, so getting this tree right on day one matters more here than almost anywhere else in the system.
  2. Lead Source master values — 10 custom sources beyond ERPNext's defaults (Website, Referral, Cold Call, Trade Show, etc. — tailor to the client's actual channels).
  3. Customer Group hierarchy — Dealer / Distributor / Direct Customer, or whatever the client's actual segmentation is.
  4. Sales Person hierarchy + Price Lists — who reports to whom, and what each tier is authorized to quote.
  5. Pipeline Stages — 10 stages configured on Opportunity's Sales Stage master.
  6. Assignment Rules — one per zone, mapping Territory to the responsible Sales Person; the system auto-creates 4 placeholder reps so this works out of the box on any fresh site, but real names should replace these before go-live.
The Page Flow — Default ERPNext, With Our Additions Threaded In

Every numbered step below is 100% native ERPNext — this exact click path works on a completely stock install with zero customization, and is what any ERPNext-trained user already knows. The purple call-outs are what scale_sales adds at that exact step, on top of the native screen — not a replacement of it. This is the real answer to "what's default vs. what did we build": read the numbered steps alone for default ERPNext; read the whole block for what actually happens on this site today.

Default flow — Lead to Cash
Navigate via the Awesomebar (top search) or Selling workspace in the left sidebar.
1
Go to Selling → Lead → New Lead
Fill Lead Name, Company Name, Email, Mobile, Source, Territory. Click Save. Native ERPNext creates the record with status "Lead."
+ scale_sales BANT fields (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) and UTM fields appear on the same form. On Save: duplicate detection runs automatically, an AI Lead Score (0–100) and Lead Priority (Hot/Warm/Cold) are computed and shown, and — if Territory matches a configured zone — the Lead auto-assigns to the right rep via an Assignment Rule, with no manual routing step.
2
On the Lead, click Create → and pick from Customer / Opportunity / Quotation / Prospect
Native ERPNext's Lead form offers 4 different things you can spawn from the same Lead. They are not steps of one sequence — they're 4 different purposes. See the box below before picking one.

🔍 Understand the difference — Customer, Opportunity, Quotation, Prospect

Customer
Converts the Lead into a proper Customer master record — name, addresses, contacts, price list, tax settings. Required before anything can ever be invoiced against this party; a Lead itself can't be quoted, ordered, or billed. Think "this is now a real account," independent of whether any deal has closed.
Opportunity
Creates one specific tracked deal — Sales Stage, Probability %, expected Items/value, Expected Closing Date — linked back to this Lead. One Lead can spawn multiple Opportunities over time (they buy once, come back a year later for something else — that's a second Opportunity, same Lead/Customer).
Quotation
A formal priced proposal — Item rows, rates, taxes, terms. You can create this directly from the Lead, skipping Opportunity entirely — Quotation's "Quotation To" field accepts Lead or Customer. The "proper" pipeline is Lead → Opportunity → Quotation, but the system doesn't force it; jump straight to a quote for simple deals.
Prospect
An account-level record (Company Name, Industry, Territory, Annual Revenue, No. of Employees) for B2B account-based selling — groups multiple Leads/Contacts under one target organization, so several people at the same company roll up under one view. Only appears in the menu while this Lead isn't already linked to a Prospect.

⚠ Naming collision to know about: scale_sales also uses the word "Prospect" — but only as one value inside the Customer Status picklist (Active / Inactive / Prospect / At Risk / Churned) and the Customer Group list. That's a status label on a Customer, completely unrelated to the native Prospect DocType described above. Verified directly in code — the native Prospect record type isn't used anywhere in this build. Don't confuse "a Customer marked status = Prospect" with "a native Prospect account record."

+ scale_sales The Customer form's "More Information" section gains 4 fields — Annual Turnover, Competitor Products Used, Account Classification (A/B/C/Key/Strategic), Customer Status (Active/At Risk/Churned). On the Opportunity, Stakeholder and Competitor child tables appear (Contacts tab); Win Probability and Next Best Action auto-recalculate on save — both rule-based on real fields today, not an LLM call.
3
On the Opportunity, click Create → Quotation
Add Item rows (Item Code, Qty, Rate), taxes, terms. Click Save, then Submit. Native buttons are Draft → Submit → Cancel.
+ scale_sales If a discount % is entered, the native Submit button is replaced by a Workflow state pill (Draft → Pending Manager Approval → Pending VP Approval → Approved). ⚠ The exact discount threshold that triggers each stage is an open, unresolved conflict — the original spec said >10% Manager-only, the signed-off Excel says >10% Manager / >20% VP, what actually shipped is ≤20% Manager / >20% VP. See sales-tracker.html — needs a decision.
4
On the Quotation, click Create → Sales Order
Confirm Items, quantities, delivery date. Save, then Submit.
No scale_sales addition at this step — pure native ERPNext.
5
On the Sales Order, click Create → Delivery Note (stock items) and/or Create → Sales Invoice
Delivery Note confirms items shipped and updates the Stock Ledger on Submit. Sales Invoice posts to the General Ledger on Submit and can be created directly from the Sales Order for service-only lines.
+ scale_sales On the Sales Invoice, More Info tab gains Collection Status and Promise-to-Pay Date — both auto-set on Submit and on payment. AR ageing buckets (30/60/90/120 days) are live and reflect this data.
6
On the Sales Invoice, click Create → Payment
Records the Payment Entry against the invoice, reconciling the outstanding amount. Native, no customization.
+ scale_sales For overdue invoices instead of a payment: open Dunning Type (Collections card on the Scale Sales workspace) to see the 3 templates (T-7 reminder, T-0 due alert, T+15 formal notice) — selected and sent manually today; automatic scheduled escalation is scoped (T19) but not yet built.
Not part of the native chain — additional screens scale_sales adds
+
Scale Sales workspace → Field Visit shortcut → New
Log a visit against a Customer with GPS check-in/check-out, captured via a mobile-optimized web form. No native ERPNext equivalent — this is a wholly new DocType, not an extension of an existing screen.
+
Scale Sales workspace → Sales Command Center
A live KPI dashboard page — Total Leads, Hot Leads, Pipeline Value, Pending Collections, Field Visits. The first screen a sales manager should open each morning.

What we need from the client

  • Existing customer master data — name, GSTIN, address, any turnover/classification data available, for migration
  • Territory / region definitions actually used by their sales team today (not assumed — confirm against the real org)
  • Existing price lists or rate cards
  • Their actual Lead source channels (website, referral, trade show, cold outreach, etc.) — the 10 shipped values are a starting template, not a fixed list
  • Sales team org chart, for accurate Territory-to-rep Assignment Rule mapping (replacing the 4 placeholder reps)
  • Their actual discount-approval policy — directly resolves the open T08 conflict above

2 · CRM

App installed: crm (Frappe CRM v1.79.1, pinned) · Full detail: sales-module.html Section A "Platform decision"

Read this before assuming "CRM" means the separate Frappe CRM app. The original architecture plan (documented in frappe-verdict.html and architecture.html) intended a specific split: Frappe CRM — a separate, modern, Vue-based app — would own Lead/Opportunity/Pipeline management, while native ERPNext Selling would take over from Quotation onward. That plan is installed on the bench (crm is in sites/apps.txt) but is not what actually got built. Verified directly in scale_sales/hooks.py's doc_events: the live system hooks into ERPNext's native Lead and Opportunity DocTypes — not Frappe CRM's separate CRM Lead / CRM Deal schema. scale_sales/TESTING.md confirms this on the ground — it instructs testers to open /app/lead (native ERPNext), never the separate CRM app.

What this means in practice

There is no separate CRM setup checklist in this manual, because the real, live pipeline system is exactly what's described in §1 Sales above — native Lead → Opportunity, extended by scale_sales. "CRM" and "the Sales pipeline" are the same system today. Frappe CRM sits installed and idle, not wired to anything scale_sales does.

Why this happened, and whether it's a problem

This isn't a bug — both the native-ERPNext build and the originally-planned Frappe-CRM build are legitimate architectures, and native ERPNext was faster to extend given scale_sales's heavy customization needs (BANT fields, territory automation, AI scoring) — those are simpler to bolt onto a DocType the team already controls fully than to bridge into a separate app's own schema. The Sales Module Plan already tracks the same underlying discrepancy under a different name — T20 "Naming Alignment" flags that Sales Stage was built on ERPNext's Opportunity stage master, not Frappe CRM's Deal Stage, for the identical reason.

If you open Frappe CRM instead — what you'd see, and why it's a dead end today

Frappe CRM is a genuinely separate app with its own URL and its own native page flow. Documenting it here so nobody mistakes it for a second, alternative way into the same data — it isn't; it's a disconnected island on this site today.

Frappe CRM's own native flow (installed, but not wired to scale_sales)
1
Open the separate Frappe CRM URL (its own app, not /app/...) → Leads → + Create
Creates a CRM Lead record — a completely different DocType from native ERPNext's Lead, with its own fields and its own Kanban board.
2
Convert to CRM Deal, drag across its own Kanban pipeline
Native Frappe CRM's own Deal Stage settings — visually similar to Opportunity's Sales Stage, but a completely separate master list, on a completely separate DocType.

🔍 Understand the difference — Sales Stage vs. Deal Stage

Sales Stage
Native ERPNext's Opportunity field — what's actually live on this site (§1 Sales, step 2). 10 stages configured for this build.
Deal Stage
Frappe CRM's own separate setting — on a record type nothing here reads or writes to. If someone hands you a pipeline-stage report and the stage names don't match what you see in §1, this is almost certainly why.
⚠ Neither of these records is visible anywhere in the Sales flow described in §1 — no BANT fields, no AI scoring, no territory auto-assignment, no Win Probability. This is genuinely a different, disconnected system with the same DB but no data bridge between the two Lead concepts.

Decision needed, not yet made

If the business wants Frappe CRM's modern Kanban interface as the actual day-to-day sales UI, that requires either (a) building a real data bridge syncing native Lead/Opportunity into Frappe CRM's Lead/Deal records, or (b) migrating scale_sales's hooks and custom fields onto Frappe CRM's DocTypes instead of ERPNext's — a genuine rebuild, not a config toggle. Until that decision is made, train users on the native ERPNext Lead/Opportunity screens described in §1, not on Frappe CRM's UI.

What we need from the client

  • None specific to this section — see §1 Sales for the actual data requirements, since that's the live system
  • If Frappe CRM's UI is genuinely wanted, that's a scoping conversation, not a data-collection one — flag to Raghib before assuming it's in scope
● In Progress — app scaffolded, hooks wired, no custom DocTypes yet

3 · HR

App: scale_hr · Full detail: hr-module.html · Lead: Vandana · Target: Sep 24, 2026

Significance

Manages the people side — hiring, attendance, leave, payroll, performance, exit. Every other module's "who did what" traces back to an Employee record here: Sales Person maps to a User/Employee, Projects' Resource Allocation reads Employee availability, Timesheet approval routes through the HR reporting structure.

Prerequisites

Golden Path items 1, 10, 11 — Company, Department, Employee master itself is what this module builds out, but Holiday List and Designation must exist before the first real Employee record is created.

Initial Setup Checklist
  1. Holiday List — per location/year, needed before Attendance and Leave calculations work correctly.
  2. Department tree — matching the client's real org structure.
  3. Designation master — job titles/grades.
  4. Employee master data import — the single most critical external-data dependency in this whole manual; every other module depends transitively on Employee existing. See below.
  5. Leave Type + Leave Policy — annual/sick/casual leave rules per the client's actual policy.
  6. Salary Structure — required before Payroll can run.
The Page Flow — Default Frappe HRMS, With Our Additions Threaded In

As of 5 Aug 2026, scale_hr has its app scaffold and doc-event/scheduler hooks wired but zero custom DocTypes built yet — so the purple call-outs below describe what will layer on, not what's live today. Every numbered step is native Frappe HRMS, usable right now with zero customization.

Default flow — Hire to Payslip
1
Go to HR → Recruitment → Job Opening → New
Define the role, then Job Applicant → New for each candidate. Save.
Planned · scale_hr AI candidate scoring reads the applicant against the Job Opening requirements and writes a fit score — not built yet.
2
On the Job Applicant, click Create → Employee Onboarding, then finalize the Employee record
Fill DOJ, Department, Designation, Salary Structure link. Save.

🔍 Understand the difference — Employee vs. User

The single most common confusion point in the whole system, because both records can share the same name and email.

Employee
The HR record — designation, department, salary structure, leave balance, reporting manager. This is what Payroll, Leave, and Appraisal all operate on.
User
The login/system account — what someone actually signs into the site with, and what permissions/roles attach to. A Customer or Supplier portal contact is also a "User" but is never an Employee.

The two are linked via the user_id field on Employee (verified directly in the schema) — but they're separate records with separate lifecycles: creating an Employee does not automatically create a login User, and a User existing doesn't mean an Employee record exists for them. This matters directly for Sales Person-to-rep mapping and Projects' Resource Allocation, both of which need to resolve "which Employee does this User correspond to."

3
Go to HR → Attendance → Leave Application / Attendance → New
Employees or managers log leave/attendance against the Employee record and Holiday List.

🔍 Understand the difference — Leave Application vs. Attendance

Leave Application
A request — "I want to be off on these dates," submitted for manager approval, checked against remaining Leave Type balance.
Attendance
The actual daily record — Present / Absent / On Leave / Work From Home, one row per employee per day. This is what Payroll actually reads to calculate pay.

These aren't independent — approving a Leave Application automatically creates or updates the matching Attendance records for those dates (verified directly in the HRMS source: Leave Application.update_attendance()). Cancelling the Leave Application removes them again. You don't need to separately mark Attendance for approved leave days — the system does it for you.

Planned · scale_hr Geo Attendance, Face Recognition check-in, and a Work From Home mode extend this same screen — none built yet.
4
Go to HR → Payroll → Salary Structure Assignment, then run Payroll Entry
Generates Salary Slips for the payroll period, posts to Accounts' GL on Submit.

🔍 Understand the difference — three ways money moves relative to an Employee

Salary Slip
Periodic payroll — the recurring, scheduled payment for time worked, generated from Salary Structure.
Employee Advance
Money given upfront — a cash float before spending happens (e.g. before a business trip), separate from payroll.
Expense Claim
Reimbursement after spending — an employee submits actual receipts, gets paid back, and it can settle against an existing Employee Advance if one was taken.

These three are also exactly the mechanism behind Accounts' Petty Cash handling — see §4 Accounts, which has no dedicated "Petty Cash" screen at all; it's this same Advance + Claim pair.

5
Go to HR → Performance → Appraisal Cycle → Appraisal → New
Score KRAs per the cycle (Monthly/Quarterly/Annual).
Planned · scale_hr AI-suggested coaching notes reading the actual KRA scores — not built yet.
6
Go to HR → Exit → Employee Separation → New
Native full exit workflow.
Planned · scale_hr Skill-gap reporting on Training Result, Grievance logging, and Travel+Claims (split from one combined native feature) all extend adjacent screens — none built yet.

How this differs from default Frappe HRMS

Frappe HRMS natively gives all of the above at a basic, functional level — this is the module with the highest native OOTB coverage after Accounts. scale_hr's job is the AI layer (candidate scoring, retention risk flags, appraisal coaching notes) plus India-specific compliance depth — PF, ESI, PT, LWF (Labour Welfare Fund), Gratuity, and Labour Registers aren't native — and workflow refinements. As of today, only the scaffolding for that layer exists — the actual custom DocTypes and AI hooks are still being built, so every native step above works exactly as described, unmodified.

What we need from the client

  • Full employee master — name, DOB, DOJ, designation, department, salary structure, PF/ESI numbers, bank details
  • Leave balances carried over from any prior system
  • Holiday calendar per location
  • Existing salary structures / CTC breakdowns
  • Statutory registration numbers — PF/ESI/PT establishment codes
● Early / Phase 0 — install + seed hooks live, demo data seeded, no custom DocTypes

4 · Accounts

App: scale_account · Full detail: accounts-module.html · Lead: Uday · Target: Sep 5, 2026

Significance

The single source of financial truth. Every Sales Invoice, Purchase Invoice, Payroll run, and Expense Claim ultimately posts here — nothing else in the system "counts" financially until it hits the General Ledger.

Prerequisites

Company and Fiscal Year (Golden Path 1–2) — this module largely is the Chart of Accounts / GL foundation the Golden Path describes, so its own prerequisite is minimal, but it must be substantially configured before Sales, Purchase, or Payroll can post real transactions correctly in production (they'll accept transactions without it in a dev/demo site, but the postings won't be financially meaningful).

Initial Setup Checklist
  1. Chart of Accounts — native template or import the client's existing CoA.
  2. Fiscal Year — if not already done in the Golden Path.
  3. Cost Center tree — shared with Finance and Projects; build once.
  4. Bank Account(s) — linked to GL accounts for reconciliation.
  5. Tax templates — via India Compliance app.
  6. Opening balances — a Journal Entry capturing the client's actual starting financial position. Critical external-data dependency — see below.
The Page Flow — Default ERPNext Accounting, With Our Additions Threaded In

This is the most native module in the whole build — confirmed directly against the local bench: scale_account has zero custom DocTypes. Nearly every step below is stock ERPNext with no addition at all.

Default flow — Setup to Statement
1
Go to Accounting → Chart of Accounts
Accept the standard template auto-created with Company, or edit/import the client's own CoA tree.
2
Go to Accounting → Journal Entry → New to post opening balances
One Journal Entry capturing the client's actual starting financial position. Save, then Submit.

🔍 Understand the difference — Journal Entry vs. Payment Entry

Payment Entry
Purpose-built for money moving against a specific reference — settling a Sales Invoice, Purchase Invoice, or Employee Advance. The default choice whenever an actual invoice/claim exists to pay against.
Journal Entry
General-purpose double-entry for everything else — opening balances, accruals, provisions, inter-account transfers, adjustments with no invoice behind them.

Rule of thumb: if you're paying/receiving against a document that already exists in the system, use Payment Entry. If you're recording something that has no such document, use Journal Entry.

3
Nothing to click — the General Ledger updates automatically
Every submitted Sales Invoice, Purchase Invoice, Payment Entry, Salary Slip, and Journal Entry across every module posts its own GL entries. This is the one step in the whole manual with literally no navigation — it just happens.
4
Go to Accounting → Bank Reconciliation Tool
Match bank statement lines against Payment Entries. Native, install India Compliance separately for GST return filing and E-Invoicing (IRN/QR) — same app, one-time install, no custom code.
5
Go to Accounting → Financial Statements → Balance Sheet / Profit and Loss
Native reports, filterable by Company/Fiscal Year/Cost Center.
+ scale_account A handful of MIS script reports and a consolidated Executive Dashboard (CFO/CXO view) sit alongside these native statements — the only genuinely custom-built pieces in this module.

🔍 Watch for this — "Petty Cash" has no dedicated screen

Someone looking for a "Petty Cash" menu item won't find one — there isn't one, by design. It's handled through Employee Advance (disbursing cash to a custodian) + Expense Claim (recording what it was spent on), the exact same pair described in §3 HR. Route petty cash questions to those two screens, not a search for something called "Petty Cash."

🔍 Watch for this — "Project Profitability" is not a real native report name

If a spec or a teammate mentions a native "Project Profitability" report, be skeptical — checked directly against the ERPNext source and confirmed no report exists by that exact name. What does exist: Profitability Analysis, an Accounts-level report filterable by Cost Center or Project as a dimension, plus project_summary and project_billing_summary under the Projects module. This exact naming gap was caught once already during the Projects module's own PDF fidelity check — see pmo-module.html.

How this differs from default ERPNext Accounting

The "custom" work here is thin by design: install India Compliance for GST/E-Invoicing, configure the CoA and Cost Centers correctly, and build the MIS reports + Executive Dashboard above. Every other screen — Chart of Accounts, Journal Entry, GL, Bank Reconciliation, Balance Sheet, P&L — is exactly what anyone trained on vanilla ERPNext Accounting already knows, unmodified.

What we need from the client

  • Opening trial balance / opening GL balances
  • Existing Chart of Accounts, if migrating from another system
  • GSTIN and full statutory registration details
  • Bank account details for reconciliation
  • Existing Fixed Asset register, if any
  • Historical AR/AP ageing, if migrating mid-year rather than at a fiscal year boundary
● Early / Phase 0 — install + seed hooks live, depends on Accounts

5 · Finance

App: scale_finance · Full detail: finance-module.html · Lead: Uday · Target: Oct 10, 2026

Significance

Turns Accounts' raw bookkeeping into forward-looking management insight — budgeting, forecasting, working capital, profitability analysis. This is the CFO's toolkit, distinct from Accounts' bookkeeper toolkit — Accounts answers "what happened," Finance answers "what's likely to happen and is it healthy."

Prerequisites

scale_account must be substantially live first. Finance has no independent data source — it reads Cost Centre, GL, and transaction data that Accounts produces. This isn't a soft dependency: Finance's reports are meaningless without real Accounts data flowing. The Page Flow — 100% Default ERPNext, Nothing Custom Yet
Default flow — Supplier to Payment
1
Go to Buying → Supplier → New

2

🔍 Understand the difference — RFQ vs. Supplier Quotation vs. Purchase Order

Three separate documents in sequence, the classic point of confusion for anyone new to ERPNext Buying:

RFQ
Your ask. "Request for Quotation" — sent to one or more Suppliers, asking them to quote on a specific item list. No commitment yet.
Supplier Quotation
Their answer. Each Supplier's response to the RFQ — their price, lead time, terms. Compare multiple side by side via the native Supplier Quotation Comparison report.
Purchase Order
Your commitment. The actual binding order, created from the winning Supplier Quotation (or directly, skipping the first two entirely for known suppliers).
3
On the Purchase Order, add Item rows, Qty, Rate, delivery date. Save, then Submit
Sends the commitment to the Supplier (email/print out natively supported).
4
When goods arrive, from the PO click Create → Purchase Receipt
Confirms quantity actually received, updates the Stock Ledger on Submit.
5
From the Purchase Receipt (or PO directly), click Create → Purchase Invoice
Native 3-way matching checks PO/Receipt/Invoice quantities and rates line up. Posts to GL on Submit.
6
From the Purchase Invoice, click Create → Payment
Records the Payment Entry to the Supplier, reconciling the outstanding amount.

8 · Manufacturing (Production)

Planned app: scale_manufacturing · Dedicated plan: manufacturing-module.html 🆕

Significance

For any organization that makes rather than only resells — converts raw materials into finished goods via BOM-driven Work Orders. The physical execution layer behind a Sales Order that requires production rather than a stock pick.

Prerequisites

Item (both raw materials and finished goods), BOM, Warehouse, Work Station, Operation master, Routing.

Initial Setup Checklist
  1. Item masters — both raw materials and finished goods
  2. Bill of Materials (BOM) — per finished good
  3. Work Station + Operation definitions
  4. Warehouse stages — WIP, stores, finished goods
The Page Flow — 100% Default ERPNext, Nothing Custom Yet
Default flow — BOM to Finished Good
1
Go to Manufacturing → Bill of Materials → New
Select the finished-good Item, add raw-material Item rows with quantities, set the default Warehouse. Save, then Submit.
2
Go to Manufacturing → Production Plan → New
Pull in Sales Orders or a manual forecast quantity; the Plan calculates raw-material requirements against current stock.

🔍 Understand the difference — BOM, Production Plan, Work Order, Job Card

Four documents at four different levels of abstraction — easy to conflate because they all sound like "the production paperwork":

BOM
The recipe. A definition — "to make 1 unit of Finished Good X, you need these raw materials in these quantities." Doesn't represent any actual production run.
Production Plan
The demand aggregation. "We need to make N units of X by date Y" — pulled from Sales Orders or forecast, calculates net material requirements against current stock.
Work Order
One specific batch to produce — released from the Production Plan, references one BOM, one quantity.
Job Card
Shop-floor execution — one per Operation on the Work Order, where actual time/quantity gets logged as work happens.
3
From the Production Plan, click Create → Work Order
One Work Order per BOM/quantity to produce. Save, then Submit to release it to the shop floor.
4
On the Work Order, Job Cards are auto-created per Operation — go to Manufacturing → Job Card
Shop-floor staff start/complete each Job Card as work happens; time and quantity are logged here.
5
From the Work Order, click Create → Stock Entry (Material Transfer for Manufacture, then Manufacture)
Issues raw materials from stores to WIP, then receives the finished good into the Finished Goods warehouse on completion.

Honest scope statement: same caveat as Buying — this module hasn't been through AIWS's own gap-analysis process yet. Every step above is vanilla ERPNext Manufacturing, pending a dedicated scope review before Wave 2 starts.

What we need from the client

  • BOM structures for existing products
  • Routing / operation time standards
  • Existing shop-floor process documentation
  • Machine / work-station capacity data — this also feeds forward into Projects' Resource Management if machines are shared across Projects and Production
