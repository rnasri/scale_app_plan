🎬 Client Demo Story

Apex Manufacturing Co — The Pump Assembly A1 Bulk Order

One realistic manufacturing project, seeded consistently across every module using nothing but Scale's own native project linking — click from the Project record straight into the real Sales Order, BOM, Work Order, Purchase Order, and Payment that belong to it.

1 The Story

Apex Manufacturing Co, an existing industrial customer, places a bulk order for 250 units of Pump Assembly A1 — a precision pump used in their water-treatment line expansion. Fulfilling it touches every part of the business: Sales books the order, PMO runs it as a real project with its own risks and budget, Manufacturing builds a Bill of Materials and runs it through Production Planning to see what's short, Purchase closes that shortfall with a new supplier, and Stock receives, tracks, and feeds the raw material back into production.

Every record below is real and already live on this bench — not mocked, not a slide. The project is PROJ-0025 · Apex Manufacturing — Pump Assembly A1 Bulk Order, and every module's data is tagged to it the same way Scale tags any project-linked transaction — which is what makes the Connections tab on the Project record able to show all of it in one place, natively, with nothing custom built to make it work.

2 The Flow, Module by Module

PMO (the hub). The Project is created first — customer Apex Manufacturing Co, a Project Manager and a 3-person team from the existing HR roster (including QA Engineer Vikram Singh, assigned directly to the QC Task — the closest existing profile match to a manufacturing quality role), four Tasks (Procure → Produce → QC → Dispatch), a Project Risk flagging steel price volatility, an Issue logging a real cosmetic-defect finding from the first production batch (traced to Assembly Line 1 die wear, resolved before dispatch), Timesheets, a Quality Inspector budget line, and an Expense Claim for a supplier quality audit visit.

Sales. A Quotation for 250 units is raised, walked through Sales' own discount-approval workflow, and converted into a real Sales Order — the commercial trigger for everything downstream.

Manufacturing. A real multi-item Bill of Materials is built for Pump Assembly A1 (steel sheet, copper wire, ball bearings). A Production Plan pulls the Sales Order through Scale's own native MRP engine, which computes a genuine raw-material shortfall against Stock's existing baseline — not a fabricated number. A Work Order is raised and submitted, which automatically creates its own Job Card, logged with a real Time Log against a real employee.

Purchase. The MRP-generated Material Request is converted into a Purchase Order with a new supplier, SteelCraft Industries, walked through Purchase's own approval workflow. Goods Receipt deliberately includes a small rejected-quantity line (showing the automatic Rejected Warehouse default), and the Purchase Invoice carries a deliberate rate deviation — automatically flagged by the 3-way match check, never blocked. A Payment Entry closes the loop.

Accounts. The mirror image of Purchase, on the revenue side. Before this leg existed, the Sales Order sat fully unbilled — a real gap, not staged. A Sales Invoice bills Apex Manufacturing Co the full ₹51,62,500, on the same "50 Adv / 50 Net15" Payment Terms Template Accounts already uses elsewhere on this bench, and a Payment Entry receives the advance half (₹25,81,250) — the Net-15 balance stays genuinely outstanding, exactly as a real part-paid invoice would. Both the invoice and the payment carry the mandatory Cost Center Accounts enforces bench-wide.

Stock. A baseline raw-material receipt exists before any of this starts — deliberately modest, so the shortfall Manufacturing's MRP finds is real, not staged.

HR. Four real touchpoints on the same 4 team members already on this project: Vikram Singh (QA) gets a Recognition Entry for the QC catch behind the Issue above; Karan Bhatt takes approved leave that overlaps the production window; a new Manufacturing Engineer hire, Ananya Verma, is mid-onboarding — the one HR record with a genuine project field, set directly to PROJ-0025; and Rohan Kapoor gets a 360 Performance Feedback from Meera Nair citing his leadership of the order, riding the same Appraisal Cycle machinery HR already runs company-wide rather than a one-off for this demo.

3 Project Walkthrough

Everything below is real, clickable, and already seeded. Open the PROJ-0025 Project and start here:

  1. Open the Open → Project. Point out the PMO Approved workflow state, the Project Manager field, and the Key Risks Summary — this is a real project, not a demo shell.
  2. Scroll to Connections. This is the whole point: one native Scale tab, zero custom code, showing every module's real linked records grouped by type — Project (Task, Timesheet, Issue), Material (Material Request, BOM, Stock Entry), Sales (Sales Order), Purchase (Purchase Order, Receipt, Invoice), Manufacture (Work Order), Risk.
  3. Click Issue (Open →) → a real QC finding from the first production batch, raised by Vikram Singh (the QA Engineer assigned to the QC Task), traced back to the same Assembly Line 1 die wear behind the Downtime Entry in the Feature Showcase below, and closed Resolved before dispatch — one paragraph that ties the PMO, Manufacturing, and HR stories together.
  4. Click Sales Order (Open →) → show the real 250-unit order to Apex Manufacturing Co, submitted, tagged to this exact project.
  5. Click BOM (Open →) → show the real multi-item bill of materials and its live costed total — not a placeholder.
  6. Click Work Order (Open →) → open its Job Card from the Connections tab there too — show the real Time Log logged against a real employee.
  7. Click Material Request (Open →) → explain this was not typed in by hand — it's the output of Scale's own native MRP engine, run against the Sales Order, computing a genuine shortfall.
  8. Click Purchase Order (Open →) → then its own Connections tab to reach the Goods Receipt and Purchase Invoice — point out the rejected-quantity line and the automatic 3-way match flag on the invoice (Flagged — rate 92.0 vs PO rate 85.0), both real built features, not staged for the demo.
  9. Back at the Project, click Sales Invoice (Open →) — it sits right in the same Sales Connections group as the Sales Order, no gap here at all. Then its own Connections tab to reach the Payment Entry — the advance half received, the Net-15 half still genuinely outstanding on the invoice. This is Accounts closing the revenue loop the same way Purchase just closed the expense loop.
  10. Close the loop back at the Project — every one of those documents is reachable from the single record you started at.
  11. HR, from the employee side. Worth saying plainly: this doesn't show up in the Connections tab above — Project's Connections list is a fixed set of doctypes Scale ships (Task/Timesheet/Issue, Sales/Purchase/Manufacture, plus a "Claims" group HRMS itself adds for Expense Claim), and HR's own doctypes aren't among them today. Only Employee Onboarding carries a genuine project field, the same class of link everything else in this walkthrough uses; the other three connect through the 4 employees already on this project's team instead. Open them directly: Recognition Entry → (Vikram Singh, the QC catch behind the Issue above), Leave Application → (Karan Bhatt, overlapping the production window), Employee Onboarding → (Ananya Verma, Manufacturing Engineer, project set directly), and 360 Feedback → (Meera Nair reviewing Rohan Kapoor's leadership of the order, off the real Appraisal → record it belongs to).

4 How This Was Built — Purely Data, Zero Code Changes

The connection mechanism is Project.project — a field Scale already ships on Sales Order, Sales Invoice, Purchase Order/Receipt/Invoice, Payment Entry, Stock Entry, Work Order, Job Card, BOM, Expense Claim, and (as of this session) Employee Onboarding. Setting it consistently on every record is the entire trick; the Connections tab and its grouping already exist natively — Sales Invoice and Payment Entry slot straight into the existing "Sales" and "Payment" groups, no gap at all. HR is the one exception: that fixed list doesn't include Employee Onboarding yet, so that one link is real but not yet Connections-visible (see chapter 3, step 11). Nothing was added to the schema, no new doctypes, no new hooks — this is a pure data-seeding exercise, one small script per app.

# Run once, in this exact order — each step depends on data the previous one creates 1. bench --site <site> execute scale_project_management.demo_project_seed.run 2. bench --site <site> execute scale_sales.demo_project_seed.run 3. bench --site <site> execute scale_stock.demo_project_seed.run 4. bench --site <site> execute scale_manufacturing.demo_project_seed.run 5. bench --site <site> execute scale_purchase.demo_project_seed.run 6. bench --site <site> execute scale_account.demo_project_seed.run 7. bench --site <site> execute scale_hr.demo_project_seed.run

Every script is idempotent (safe to re-run), manual-invoke only (never wired into after_install/after_migrate — a fresh site migrating for the first time doesn't have the masters this assumes, the same reason every other demo seed in this codebase stays manual), and independently safe: each app commits only its own data at its own end, so one script failing partway can't corrupt another app's data. Accounts' own script (step 6) reuses the real "50 Adv / 50 Net15" Payment Terms Template this app's own seed already created rather than inventing one, and caught a real bug before it landed: applying that template splits get_payment_entry()'s result into 2 reference rows against the same invoice, so naively allocating "half" to both double-counted the total and failed Payment Entry's own "Difference Amount must be zero" check — fixed by keeping only the earlier-due row. HR's own script (step 7) reuses this bench's existing Appraisal Cycle/Template rather than creating a second one, and deliberately never submits its Employee Onboarding record — that doctype's own on_submit() auto-creates a decoy Project and silently overwrites the real one, a gotcha caught by reading the controller before building rather than after. Shipped as its own small, separately-reviewable PR — feature/connected-demo-data — after the three new Supply Chain apps (Stock/Purchase/Manufacturing) land, since this depends on all of them existing.

scale_finance was evaluated too and deliberately left out. All 6 of its doctypes (Investment Proposal, Loan Liability/EMI Schedule, MSME Application/Scheme/Checklist) operate at company level — none carry a project field, and by nature none tie to a specific customer order the way a loan or an MSME scheme wouldn't. Forcing a link there would mean fabricating a connection that doesn't reflect what the module actually does, which this whole thread has deliberately avoided everywhere else — so this is a documented "no," not an oversight.

5 Feature Showcase — Every Concept, Simple to Complex

Everything above tells one project's story. The records below are different — each is a standalone, self-contained example built specifically to explain one concept on its own, in plain language, with a real record to open — Manufacturing, Stock, and Purchase first, then HR, Accounts, and Finance. They don't belong to the Apex Manufacturing project (a couple could, most deliberately don't) — think of this as a walk-through library, not a second story. Start at the top of each module and work down; each one gets a little more involved than the last.

🏭 Manufacturing

The build order below mirrors how a real manufacturing team actually grows into the module — starting from "can I describe what a product is made of" and ending at "can I outsource part of production and still trust the costing."

📦 Stock

Stock underpins both Manufacturing and Purchase — Item and Warehouse masters, batch/expiry tracking, and physical counting are the foundation everything else in this demo depends on.

Batch Tracking with Expiry Simple
