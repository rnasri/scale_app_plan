Purchase & Procurement Module — Implementation Plan

Procure-to-Pay for scale_purchase. Not started — pre-build plan (feature map + task list). Recommended as the second module to build, right after Stores.

Naming decision: ERPNext ships this natively as the "Buying" module. Per the client's own scope document and PRD, we name it Purchase & Procurement everywhere on the portal, in the app title, and in any client-facing material — Buying stays only as Frappe's internal module label under the hood.

App: scale_purchase, own app, required_apps = [..., "scale_stock"] — declares the dependency explicitly rather than bundling into one shared app, so a trading/distribution client can install Stores + Purchase without Manufacturing.

Why Purchase goes second: Its only real dependency is Stores' Item/Warehouse masters (Purchase Order → Goods Receipt has to post against something). Everything else here is self-contained. It also has the richest source material of the three new modules — a dedicated 15-page client PRD (Purchase & Procurement Management Module.pdf, in Claude/reference_from_team/) in addition to the Excel scope sheet, with a fully specified AI Procurement Copilot and CXO dashboard. Manufacturing, by contrast, only has Excel one-liners — building Purchase now, while its spec is freshest and most complete, is the better use of sequencing.

Module Objective

Give full visibility across Purchase Requisition → Supplier Selection → Purchase Order → Goods Receipt → Invoice → Payment → Supplier Performance Analysis, so the organisation buys the right material from the right supplier at the right price, quality, and time — standardising process, reducing cost and stock-outs, and giving Purchase/Finance leadership AI-assisted procurement decisions rather than spreadsheet guesswork.

Phase-by-Phase Implementation

Three phases, following the procure-to-pay lifecycle itself: Phase 1 is the transactional backbone (nothing else matters until PR→PO→GRN→Invoice works end to end). Phase 2 is sourcing/compliance/governance layered on top. Phase 3 is planning, analytics, and the AI Copilot — genuinely valuable but not required for a first working Purchase flow.

Phase 1 — Procure-to-Pay Backbone

Refine PUR-01 · Purchase Requisition (Material Request) ▶

What already works: Native Material Request DocType (Purchase / Transfer / Manufacture / Customer Provided types), linkable to Cost Centre, Project, and Item, with a mandatory Required-By date.

What to add (per PRD §1):

  • Custom fields: budget_reference, justification if not already covered by native description/attachments
  • Frappe Workflow: Employee → Department Head → Purchase Manager → Finance → Management (value-based) — matches the PRD's exact approval chain
  • Server-side validation on submit: available budget (needs PUR-12), existing stock (native projected_qty check), pending POs for the same item, minimum order quantity
AI opportunity (PRD): predict material requirement from historical consumption, suggest preferred suppliers, recommend quantity, detect duplicate requisitions, predict procurement urgency. Reports: Pending Requisitions, Department-wise PR, Budget vs Requested, PR Ageing.
OOTB PUR-02 · Supplier / Vendor Management ▶

What already works: Supplier master with Supplier Group, GST/PAN, Bank Details, Contact/Address, Payment Terms, and complete transaction history (POs, Receipts, Invoices) rolled up per supplier natively.

What to add: Custom fields for Certifications and Service Areas if the client needs them beyond native Supplier Group + territory; confirm against PRD's "Store: Company Details / GST / PAN / Bank Details / Product Categories / Certifications / Contact Persons / Credit Terms / Payment Terms / Contracts / Service Areas" list feature-by-feature before assuming anything is missing.

OOTB PUR-03 · Purchase Order Management ▶

What already works: Full PO lifecycle — create from Material Request / RFQ / Blanket Order / manual entry; Item and Service Purchase Orders; amendment control; received/billed status tracking; amount-based approval via Frappe Workflow.

What to add: Confirm the approval matrix thresholds with the client (PRD: "Approval Matrix based on PO value") and wire as a native Workflow, same pattern as Sales Quotation's discount-based routing.

AI opportunity (PRD): recommend PO consolidation, suggest delivery schedules, detect duplicate POs, recommend order quantities, predict supplier fulfillment probability.
OOTB PUR-04 · Goods Receipt (GRN) ▶

What already works: Purchase Receipt validated against the PO — received/accepted/rejected quantity, batch number, expiry date, target warehouse, and incoming Quality Inspection, all native. Supports partial receipt, multiple deliveries, over/under delivery.

Depends on: Stores warehouse configuration (STR-02) must exist first — this is the one hard dependency between the two modules.

AI opportunity (PRD): predict receiving delays, detect unusual quantity variations, recommend warehouse allocation.
Refine PUR-05 · Invoice Matching (3-way match) ▶

Approach: Native Purchase Invoice already links to PO and Purchase Receipt. Add a validate server script that flags — not silently blocks — quantity and rate deviations between PO, GRN, and Invoice before payment is released, per PRD's "quantity and rate deviations flagged before money moves."

Phase 2 — Sourcing, Compliance & Governance

OOTB PUR-06 · RFQ / Tendering ▶

What already works: Native Request for Quotation to multiple suppliers, portal self-service quotation submission, responses converting to draft Supplier Quotation. Native side-by-side comparison view (price, delivery, payment terms).

What to add: Confirm Single/Multi-vendor/Online/Email RFQ modes all match PRD requirements; the technical-specifications and warranty/quality-rating comparison criteria in PRD §2 may need a custom comparison Script Report if native comparison doesn't surface all of them.

AI opportunity (PRD): recommend vendors, predict best supplier, detect abnormal pricing, recommend negotiation points, identify procurement risks.
Refine PUR-07 · Approval Workflows ▶

Approach: One consistent Frappe Workflow pattern (amount- and category-based routing) applied across Requisition, PO, and Invoice — not three separate mechanisms. Every transition is audited natively via Frappe's Version/Workflow log.

Refine PUR-08 · Supplier Performance Management ▶

What already works: Native Supplier Scorecard — quality/delivery/responsiveness criteria, periodic standings, and standings can automatically block RFQs/POs.

What to add: Confirm the scorecard's criteria set covers PRD §6's full list (Quality, Delivery, Cost, Responsiveness, Compliance, Documentation, Service Support) — extend the native scorecard's variable set rather than building a parallel DocType.

AI opportunity (PRD): predict future supplier performance, identify high-risk suppliers, recommend supplier development plans, detect performance decline.
OOTB PUR-09 · Contract & Rate Agreement Management ▶

What already works: Native Blanket Order — agreed quantity/rate/validity, with PO releases drawing against it automatically.

What to add: Confirm SLA/Penalties/Renewal-alert fields from PRD §7 exist on Blanket Order or need custom fields; a Notification Rule for contract-expiry alerts (native scheduler).

OOTB PUR-10 · Advance Payments ▶

What already works: Native advance Payment Entry against a PO, auto-adjusted in the final Purchase Invoice; supplier-wise exposure visible in the standard Accounts Payable report. No new build expected — coordinate with Accounts.

Phase 3 — Planning, Compliance Extensions, Analytics & AI

Refine PUR-11 · Import Management ▶

What already works: Native Landed Cost Voucher spreads freight/customs/insurance into item cost; multi-currency PO/Invoice is native.

What to add: India-specific import documentation tracking (Bill of Entry, import licences) — likely a small custom child table on Purchase Receipt or a lightweight Import Document Tracker DocType, only if the client actually imports (flag as an open question — not yet confirmed either way).

Custom PUR-12 · Purchase Budget Management ▶

Approach (PRD §8): Manage budgets by Department / Cost Centre / Project / Branch; track Budget, Purchase Commitments, Actual Spend, Balance Budget. ERPNext's native Budget DocType (against Cost Centre + Account) is close but doesn't track PO-stage commitments the way the PRD wants — likely needs a thin custom layer (a Script Report plus a computed "committed spend" field) rather than a full new DocType. Feeds PUR-01's requisition-time budget check.

AI opportunity (PRD): predict budget overruns, recommend budget reallocation, identify excessive purchasing.
Refine PUR-13 · Inventory Procurement Planning ▶

Approach: Builds directly on Stores' native Reorder Level / Safety Stock / Min-Max engine (STR-09/STR-13), extended with Lead Time and EOQ (Economic Order Quantity) fields, and linked to Manufacturing's MRP output (material shortfalls from Production Planning auto-raise Material Requests here — this is the direct link to Manufacturing).

AI opportunity (PRD): demand forecasting, auto replenishment, inventory optimization, overstock detection, stock-out prediction.
Refine PUR-14 · Vendor Portal (self-service) ▶

What already works: Native portal quotation submission for RFQs.

What to add: Extend the portal with PO acknowledgment and supplier invoice submission — both are standard Frappe portal/Web Form patterns, no new architecture needed.

Refine PUR-15 · Procurement Analytics ▶

What already works: Native spend/trend reports.

What to add: Script Reports for Spend Analysis, Purchase Trend, Vendor Analysis, Price Comparison (Excel #14) — feeding the shared Dashboards module (Wave 4) rather than a Purchase-only dashboard, consistent with how every other module's analytics are structured.

AI PUR-16 · AI Procurement Copilot + Executive (CXO) Dashboard ▶

Per PRD §11 — the Copilot should: recommend preferred suppliers based on price/quality/delivery history; predict stock shortages before they occur; identify bulk-purchasing/volume-discount opportunities; detect duplicate or unnecessary purchase requests; forecast procurement demand from historical consumption + production plans; recommend vendor negotiation points from market price trends; detect procurement fraud, duplicate invoices, and unusual purchasing behaviour; predict supplier delivery delays and suggest alternates; optimise budgets by identifying savings opportunities; provide a daily procurement health summary for the Purchase Head and CFO.

Executive Dashboard (CXO View) — real-time visibility into: Total Procurement Spend, Budget vs Actual, Open PRs, Pending RFQs, POs Pending Approval, Goods in Transit, Supplier Performance Index, Purchase Cycle Time, Inventory Replenishment Status, Cost Savings Achieved, Contract Renewal Alerts, Top Supplier Risks, Procurement Compliance Score, AI Procurement Health Score, Top AI Recommendations.

Same pattern as Sales' AI layer (ai_utils.py single entry point, owned by Veeren's AI track) and PMO's Scale Intelligence sidebar assistant — this is not a new AI architecture, it's the same one extended with Purchase-scoped tools/prompts once the transactional data (PR/PO/GRN/Invoice) exists to reason over.

Task List

One task per feature group, in build order. No dates assigned yet (see Open Decisions).

PUR-01

Purchase Requisition + approval workflow

✅ Done
Effort: Medium · Depends on: Stores STR-01 (Item master)

Native Material Request + 5 new fields (Department, Priority, Budget Reference, Estimated Value, Justification) + a Frappe Workflow matching the PRD's exact chain: Employee → Department Head → Purchase Manager → Finance → Management (value-based, ₹1L placeholder threshold — confirm real number with client). Tested live: low-value path (skips Management), high-value path (routes through Management), and a reject path — all correct, including Approved actually submitting the document. Duplicate-requisition and budget-on-file soft warnings also tested live. Real bug caught and fixed: Workflow States/Actions are themselves Link fields to master doctypes that the Desk UI auto-creates invisibly — creating a Workflow via the API directly threw LinkValidationError until those masters were created explicitly first. A second bug (the current draft's own name appearing in its "possible duplicate" list) was also caught and fixed the same session.

PUR-02

Supplier master configuration

✅ Done
Effort: Low

Confirmed field-by-field against PRD §3: Company Details/GST/PAN/Bank Details/Contact Persons/Payment Terms all already native (GST/PAN via india_compliance). Added the 3 genuinely missing fields — Product Categories, Certifications, Service Areas. "Contracts" got no new field — Blanket Order already links to Supplier and is browsable filtered by it, so a duplicate field would just be a stale copy. Tested live.

PUR-03

Purchase Order lifecycle + approval matrix

✅ Done
Effort: Medium · Depends on: PUR-01

Confirmed native: creation from all 4 sources (Material Request/RFQ/Blanket Order/manual — all real links on Purchase Order Item), Item + Service POs, Blanket/Annual Rate Contracts, and PO Value/Delivery/Invoice/Payment Status tracking (grand_total/per_received/per_billed/status). Built the one real gap — a value-based approval Workflow (Purchase Manager → Finance → Management, on native grand_total, ₹5L placeholder threshold) — reusing PUR-01's exact pattern, one tier shorter. Tested live: low-value, high-value (routes through Management), and reject paths, all correct on the first migrate this time.

PUR-04

Goods Receipt configuration

✅ Done
Effort: Low · Depends on: Stores STR-02/03 (warehouse), PUR-03

Confirmed ~100% native against PRD §5: PO Reference, Received/Accepted/Rejected Qty, Batch Number, Warehouse, and even a dedicated rejected_warehouse field all already exist. The one real gap: no default for which warehouse rejected quantity lands in (checked Stock Settings and Buying Settings directly, neither has one) — wired to Stores' own "Rejected Stock" warehouse. Real bug caught and fixed: the header-level default alone wasn't enough — ERPNext validates rejected_warehouse per item row too, and a first attempt using a validate hook fired one step too late (native validation already ran); fixed by moving to before_validate. Tested live end-to-end: PO → Purchase Receipt with partial accept/reject → rejected qty confirmed landing in the correct warehouse.

PUR-05

3-way invoice match validation

✅ Done
Effort: Medium · New server script · Depends on: PUR-03, PUR-04

Sourced from the Excel scope sheet ("Purchase Invoice validated against order and receipt — quantity and rate deviations flagged before money moves") — the PDF has no dedicated Invoice Matching section, confirmed by reading all 11 of its numbered sections before writing any code. Added a validate hook on Purchase Invoice comparing each line against its linked Purchase Receipt (preferred, the actual accepted qty/rate) or Purchase Order line, for two deviation classes: rate mismatch, and invoiced qty exceeding what was received/ordered (under-qty is normal partial billing, not flagged). Two new read-only fields (Match Status, Match Deviations) persist the result — never blocks save or submit. Real bug caught and fixed, more consequential than most: native ERPNext's own rate check (Buying Settings "Maintain Same Rate") was live on this bench set to Stop — a hard block inside erpnext's own controller that runs before this app's hook ever gets a chance to fire, directly contradicting the PRD's "flagged, not silently blocked" requirement. Fixed at the settings level (Stop → Warn) rather than working around it, so native and this app's own persisted flag now agree. Also confirmed native's separate "Over Billing Allowance" check (which only compares against the PO, not the actual GRN-accepted quantity) doesn't interfere — that's the real gap this feature closes. Tested live: exact match, rate deviation, over-quantity (GRN accepted less than invoiced), and under-quantity (correctly NOT flagged) — all four cases correct.

PUR-06

RFQ & comparison

✅ Done
Effort: Medium

Confirmed native: multi-vendor RFQ, Online/Email RFQ, Technical Specifications (native item description), and Price/Delivery comparison (native Supplier Quotation Comparison report). Added the 2 genuinely missing fields on Supplier Quotation — Payment Terms, Warranty — and a new Vendor Comparison report layering both in plus Supplier Scorecard's Quality Rating. Tested live: RFQ to 2 vendors → 2 Supplier Quotations → report correctly compared both side by side, sorted by rate.

PUR-07

Approval workflow consolidation

✅ Pattern established
Effort: Low

Not a separate build — PUR-01 and PUR-03 already prove out one consistent Workflow pattern (value-based, native masters pre-created, Approved = native submit) applied to Requisition and PO. PUR-05's Invoice matching turned out not to need this pattern at all — it's a flag-only check, not a state-machine approval, since the PRD explicitly wants deviations surfaced for review rather than routed through an approval chain.

PUR-08

Supplier Performance Management

✅ Done
Effort: Low-Medium

Confirmed native Supplier Scorecard already covers Quality/Delivery/Cost/Responsiveness via formula-based criteria on real transactional variables (Total Days Late, Rejected/Accepted Amount, RFQ/SQ response days, etc.) — but the Criteria schema is formula-only, with no manual/qualitative scoring path at all, confirmed by reading it directly. So Compliance, Documentation, and Service Support (PRD §6's other 3 criteria, which have no transactional data to formula against) got 3 new manual 0-100 scores on Supplier instead. New Supplier KPI Dashboard report consolidates PRD §6's 4 named reports (KPI Dashboard/Vendor Ranking/Delivery Score/Quality Score — same reasoning as Vendor Comparison, PUR-06) into one, blending native scorecard score with the 3 manual scores; sorting it by Overall Score is Vendor Ranking. Real bug caught and fixed: Supplier Scorecard's native supplier_score is a Data field (string, not numeric) — summing it unconverted against the new Int scores threw a live TypeError; fixed with flt(). Also had to explicitly document a "0 = not yet scored" convention on the 3 new fields, since Int custom fields are NOT NULL default 0 in MariaDB — an unscored supplier and a genuine 0/100 are otherwise indistinguishable (same class of issue as Cycle Count Sheet's is_counted flag, STR-08). Tested live: a supplier with all 4 scores present, a supplier with a genuinely missing one (blend excludes it, doesn't zero it), both blended correctly.

PUR-09

Contract & Rate Agreement Management

✅ Done
Effort: Low

Confirmed native Blanket Order already has Start/Expiry Date (from_date/to_date) and per-item Rate (Pricing Terms) — read the full field list to be sure. Genuinely missing per PRD §7: Payment Terms, SLA, Penalties, and any renewal-alert mechanism. Added all 3 fields plus a native "Days Before" Notification firing 30 days before Expiry Date to the Purchase Manager role — same pattern as Stores' batch-expiry alert (STR-08). New Contract Rate Agreement Dashboard report consolidates PRD §7's 3 named reports (Contract Expiry/Active Contracts/Rate Agreement Dashboard) into one, bucketing each contract into Active / Renewal Due (≤30 days) / Expired. Tested live: a near-expiry contract correctly bucketed "Renewal Due," a far-out one "Active," and the Notification's Days Before/date_changed/role config all verified.

PUR-10

Advance payment configuration

✅ Done
Effort: Low · Coordinate with Accounts

Confirmed live, not just assumed: advance Payment Entry against a PO works natively (updates Purchase Order.advance_paid correctly on submit), and the standard Accounts Payable report correctly shows supplier-wise exposure once an invoice picks the advance up. Real gap found: "auto-adjusted in the final invoice" is not actually automatic out of the box — Purchase Invoice's own "Set Advances and Allocate (FIFO)" checkbox (allocate_advances_automatically) defaults to unticked, so a fresh invoice against that PO leaves the advance completely unallocated (0 rows in the Advances table, outstanding shows the full amount) unless someone manually ticks it or clicks "Get Advances Paid" every single time. Confirmed with a live A/B test: identical invoice, only that one field differed. Fixed with a Property Setter flipping the field's own default — no new field, no new code path — so every new Purchase Invoice now starts pre-ticked and the advance auto-pulls exactly as the PRD describes. Tested live end-to-end: PO → advance Payment Entry → new invoice (no manual step) → advance auto-allocated, outstanding correctly reduced on submit, and the standard Accounts Payable report showed the right invoiced/paid/outstanding split for the supplier.

PUR-11

Import documentation

✅ Done
Effort: Low · Owner confirmed the client does import

The plan originally assumed this would need a new custom DocType/child table for Bill of Entry tracking — wrong, confirmed by reading the actual schema. india_compliance (already installed on this bench for GST/PAN elsewhere, PUR-02) ships a full, submittable native Bill of Entry doctype: BOE No./Date, Port Code, Bill of Lading No., and a complete per-item customs duty/GST breakdown, linked back to Purchase Invoice via "Get Items From Purchase Invoice." Landed Cost Voucher (freight/customs/insurance into item cost) and multi-currency PO/Invoice were already confirmed native, as planned. The one real gap, confirmed by grepping the entire codebase for "import licence/license": zero hits anywhere — nothing tracked import licences (Advance Authorization/EPCG/DFIA) at all. Added 3 fields (Type/No./Validity) directly on the native Bill of Entry rather than a new DocType, matching this app's own "extend native, don't build parallel" convention throughout. Tested live end-to-end: a real Bill of Entry created against a real submitted Purchase Invoice, with GST tax rows and customs accounts, all 3 new fields saving and persisting correctly.

PUR-12

Purchase Budget tracking

✅ Done
Effort: Medium · Thin custom layer over native Budget

PRD §8 wants budgets by Department/Cost Centre/Project/Branch. Native Budget.budget_against only offers Cost Centre/Project — but confirmed by reading accounting_dimension.py directly: ERPNext's Accounting Dimension feature adds any doctype as a first-class budget_against option purely via configuration. One Accounting Dimension record for Department auto-created the field on Budget/Purchase Order/Purchase Invoice/GL Entry and patched Budget's own dropdown to include "Department" — no custom field, no new DocType. Branch deliberately not wired the same way: checked live, this system has zero real Branch records today, so it wasn't built for a case that doesn't exist yet — same mechanism can add it in minutes if that changes. The one real gap: native enforces PO-stage budget overspend live (its own control-action setting) but has no report showing "how much is currently committed" as a number — the native Budget Variance Report only shows Budget vs booked Actual. New Purchase Budget vs Actual report consolidates PRD §8's 3 named reports (Purchase Budget vs Actual/Department Spend/Budget Utilization) into one — filtering/sorting it by "Against" already gives the Department Spend view, Utilization % is its own column. Commitments use the exact same formula as ERPNext's own internal budget-check code (open, not-fully-billed PO amount), computed live on every report run rather than a cached field, avoiding staleness for no real benefit at this data volume. Real nuance confirmed live, not assumed: a stock item's Purchase Invoice books to "Stock Received But Not Billed," not the nominal expense account — so Budget-vs-Actual only ever reflects real expense-type (non-stock) spend, which is correct native accounting behaviour, not a gap. Tested live end-to-end: a Department-scoped Budget, a stock-item PO (commitment counted, then correctly cleared on full billing), and a genuine service-item invoice (actual spend picked up correctly).

PUR-13

Inventory procurement planning link

🔓 Unblocked — ready to resume
Effort: Medium · Depends on: Stores STR-09/13, Manufacturing MFG-15 (Done)

Extend Reorder/Min-Max with Lead Time + EOQ; wire MRP shortfall output from Manufacturing into Material Request creation. Was deliberately parked, not skipped by accident — scale_manufacturing didn't exist yet when every other Purchase feature was built (PUR-01–12/14/15 all done; only PUR-16, the AI track, remains separately deferred). The blocker is now cleared: Manufacturing's MFG-15 confirmed live that a Sales Order shortfall correctly flows through Production Plan → MRP → a real Purchase-type Material Request in "Pending" status, the exact input this feature needs. Not yet built — next real Purchase session should pick this up first.

PUR-14

Vendor Portal extension

✅ Done
Effort: Low-Medium

Real bug found by reading native code before building anything: ERPNext already renders a "Make Purchase Invoice" button on a supplier's own Purchase Order portal page, mapping it into a real draft Purchase Invoice — looks exactly like "supplier invoice submission" already exists. It doesn't work for a genuine supplier: the button's visibility check is frappe.has_permission("Purchase Invoice", "create"), an internal Desk permission a plain "Supplier" role never holds, so it silently never renders for the actual target audience. The mapping function's own permission check is correct; only the button-visibility gate in core is wrong — and this project doesn't fork core to patch it. It also never captures the supplier's own invoice number/date/document either way. Fixed with two new Web Forms + one new lightweight DocType, kept off the real Purchase Invoice entirely: PO Acknowledgment (2 new fields on native Purchase Order, scoped to each supplier's own POs for free via the same permission mechanism the native portal already uses — verified live with two real portal-user sessions, confirmed cross-supplier access is genuinely blocked) and Supplier Invoice Submission (new DocType capturing what native never did — invoice no./date/amount + an attached copy — server-side verified against the caller's own Supplier, reviewed internally with a "Convert to Purchase Invoice" button reusing the same native mapping function). Two more real bugs caught live during testing: the acknowledgment fields needed allow_on_submit (acknowledgment only ever happens post-submit; Frappe's own UpdateAfterSubmitError blocked it without that), and the timestamp hook had to be registered under before_update_after_submit, not just validate — Frappe calls the former INSTEAD of the latter once docstatus=1 and an allow_on_submit field changes. Also found the native PO→Invoice mapping doesn't propagate a header-level cost centre, defaulted to the source PO's own item cost centre instead.

PUR-15

Procurement analytics reports

✅ Done
Effort: Medium → Low, once native coverage was confirmed

Confirmed by reading native ERPNext's own Purchase Analytics report directly before writing anything: it already gives a month-by-month matrix of qty/amount by Supplier Group/Supplier/Item Group/Item across PO/Receipt/Invoice — that single native report already covers 3 of PRD §10's 4 named reports (Spend Analysis, Purchase Trend, Vendor Analysis). The one real gap: nothing anywhere compares the rate actually paid for the same item across different suppliers/orders/time — distinct from PUR-06's Vendor Comparison, which is RFQ-stage quotes before an order is placed, not rates actually paid post-award. New Price Comparison report shows, per purchase line, the item's average rate across the filtered range and each row's deviation from it, so price creep or a cheaper alternate supplier stands out immediately. Cost Savings/Procurement ROI (also named in PRD §10's broader track list, but not this plan's own narrower "what to add" line) deliberately not built — no formula for either is defined anywhere, and inventing one would be unrequested scope. Tested live: 3 real POs for the same item at different rates/suppliers, verified the report's arithmetic is internally consistent rather than assuming an exact average (other same-day test data for the same item legitimately exists on this bench from earlier sessions).
